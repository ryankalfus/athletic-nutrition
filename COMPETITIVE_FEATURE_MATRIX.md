## Evidence notes

- **MyFitnessPal** is the benchmark for a broad food database and convenient logging, not the product Nourally should imitate feature-for-feature.
- **Fueling Champions** publicly emphasizes student schedules, meal timing, reminders, visual guides, and parent/team use cases.
- **ZoneIn** publicly describes meal-specific recommendations and hydration that adjust with training schedules and biometrics.
- **MaxCoach** publicly targets teen athletes and describes age/sport/training personalization, AI coaching, parent sharing, and grocery lists.
- **AthlEAT** publicly targets youth athletes and families and describes meals around practices, games, tournaments, schedules, and dietary restrictions.

Public claims and availability can change. This matrix is a strategic comparison, not a hands-on product test.

## Strategic conclusion

Schedule awareness alone is not differentiated; several direct competitors already claim it. Nourally’s strongest wedge is the combination of:

1. **Immediate context:** what to do now based on minutes until or since activity.
2. **School-day reality:** lunch and snack windows, cafeteria access, storage, heating, classroom rules, and commute time.
3. **Execution constraints:** budget, dietary needs, packed food, available sources, and family preparation.
4. **Travel readiness:** home/away status, travel time, portability, and “pack before you go” prompts.
5. **Safer teen framing:** education and practical examples without independently prescribed calorie targets.

## Positioning statement

> For student-athletes and families who struggle to fit food into the handoffs between school and sport, Nourally is a daily fueling-logistics assistant that turns the real schedule and real constraints into an achievable next action. Unlike generic trackers and macro-first athlete plans, it starts with what is possible now—where the athlete is, what food is available, how much time remains, and what must be packed next.
